## Key Chemical Reactions in Cooking

### 1. Maillard Reaction

The Maillard reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars creates hundreds of flavor compounds.

| Temperature | Compounds Formed | Flavor Profile |
|-------------|------------------|----------------|
| 140-165°C | Pyrazines, furans | Nutty, toasted, bread-like |
| 165-180°C | Aldehydes, ketones | Caramel, roasted |
| 180-200°C | Melanoidins | Dark brown, savory |

**Ingredients that enhance Maillard:**
- Onions (amino acids + sugars)
- Garlic (sulfur compounds)
- Meat extracts
- Soy sauce (glutamates)

---

### 2. Caramelization

Sugar oxidation at high temperatures (160-200°C) without amino acids.

| Sugar Type | Compounds | Flavor Notes |
|------------|-----------|-------------|
| Sucrose | Hydroxymethylfurfural | Butterscotch, caramel |
| Glucose | Maltol | Candy-like sweetness |
| Fructose | Furans | Fruitier caramel |

---

### 3. Lipid Oxidation

Fats breaking down into aldehydes, ketones, and alcohols.

| Fat Source | Key Compounds | Flavor |
|------------|---------------|--------|
| Vegetable oils | Propanal, hexanal | Grassy, paint-like |
| Animal fat | Alkanals, alkenals | Rich, savory |
| Olive oil | Esters | Fruity, peppery |

## Flavor Compounds by Ingredient

### Alliums (Onion Family)

| Compound | Source | Flavor Characteristic |
|----------|--------|------------------------|
| Allicin | Garlic (crushed) | Pungent, sulfurous |
| Diallyl disulfide | Onion (chopped) | Onion breath, savory |
| Thiosulfinates | Raw alliums | Sharp, hot |
| Methyl cysteine sulfoxide | Cooked alliums | Sweet, mellow |

**Cooking Effect:** Heat inactivates the enzyme alliinase, stopping allicin formation. Microwaving destroys 90% of flavor compounds.

---

### Capsaicin (Chili Peppers)

| Scoville Units | Compound | Application |
|----------------|----------|-------------|
| 100-2,500 | CH-19 Sweet | Paprika, mild heat |
| 2,500-8,000 | Santaka | Asian cooking |
| 8,000-30,000 | Jalapeño | Medium heat |
| 30,000-50,000 | Cayenne | Hot |
| 100,000+ | Habanero | Extreme heat |

**Chemistry:** Capsaicin binds to TRPV1 receptors, creating the sensation of heat without actual temperature change.

### Umami Compounds

| Compound | Source | Concentration |
|----------|--------|--------------|
| Glutamate | Tomatoes, cheese, meat | 0.1-2.0% |
| GMP | Dried mushrooms, bonito | 0.01-0.05% |
| IMP | Meat, fish | 0.01-0.02% |
| Aspartate | Parmesan, soy sauce | 0.05-0.5% |

**Synergy:** GMP + Glutamate = 3x umami intensity (taste synergy)

## Cooking Reactions Reference

### Protein Denaturation

| Temperature | Protein | Effect |
|-------------|---------|--------|
| 57-63°C | Myosin | Gel formation begins |
| 63-68°C | Actin | Final gel setting |
| 70-80°C | Collagen | Tenderization |
| 100°C+ | большинство белков | Denaturation complete |

## Molecular Gastronomy Techniques

### Spherification
- Sodium alginate + Calcium chloride
- Creates gel spheres from liquids

### Emulsification
- Lecithin (egg yolk) stabilizes oil-water mixtures
- 1% concentration effective

### Foams
- Methyl cellulose for hot foams
- Lecithin for cold foams

## Chemical Changes by Cooking Method

| Method | Primary Reactions | Temperature |
|--------|-------------------|-------------|
| Boiling | Hydration, leaching | 100°C |
| Steaming | gentle starch gelatinization | 100°C |
| Sautéing | Maillard, lipid oxidation | 120-160°C |
| Frying | Maillard + caramelization | 150-200°C |
| Grilling | Maillard, charring | 200-300°C |
| Baking | Maillard, caramelization | 150-200°C |
| Smoking | Phenolic compounds | 20-80°C |
